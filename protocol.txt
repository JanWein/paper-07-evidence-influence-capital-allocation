PAPER 07 | CENTRAL REVIEW R3 | PRE-ANALYSIS AND FEASIBILITY PROTOCOL
17 October 2026. Proposed study only; not preregistered, recruited, authorized or run.
This replaces the previous verification-by-sponsor-visibility core design. The core factorial now crosses factual verification with enforceable metric governance. An optional sponsor-visibility study is separate.

1. Population, registration and scope
Target repeated small or medium discretionary investments. A durable opportunity register is established before formal business-case submission and before assignment. It includes intended objective, owner, preliminary scope, decision window and existing capital envelope. Preserve withdrawn, merged, resubmitted and rejected opportunities. Parent/child IDs prevent duplicate counting. Mandatory register coverage is an institutional prerequisite, not a feature assumed to hold in any existing firm.
The model has a one-project margin against a fallback. The proposed field trial has competing proposals and budgets; it tests a policy interaction, not every prediction of the one-project game. Capital scarcity, variation in implementation capacity and genuine metric heterogeneity may overturn its exact mixed-equilibrium formulas.
Exclude mandatory, safety-critical and urgent investments from any potentially delaying allocation randomization. Do not treat them as suitable lottery candidates. Do not change exclusions based on assigned treatment or final score.

2. Unit and estimands before method
Randomization and primary analysis unit: independent budget cluster covering all mutually competing projects and all relevant evaluation rounds. Define clusters before assignment. If managers or committees carry the procedure across rounds, assign persistent business-unit clusters rather than repeatedly randomizing dependent cohorts. Shared firm-wide binding capital pools can defeat isolation and require a broader design.
Economic target: the average difference in total risk-adjusted project value net of information-production, preparation, evaluation, audit and metric-governance costs, per predetermined budget, under two implemented policies. Distinguish entry, funding, allocated scale and realized returns. Investment outlays already in project net value are not charged again as policy costs.
Directly measured endpoint: 24-month discounted incremental portfolio cash flow less resource costs, using a frozen no-project baseline and valuation rule. It is narrower than lifetime risk-adjusted value. Freeze discounting and any observable risk-pricing kernel before outcomes. Report a conventional discounted-cash-flow endpoint if the risk kernel cannot be justified. Independently assess baseline validity; sponsor forecasts are not ground truth. Predeclare accounting for unspent capital and alternative financing. Follow canceled funded projects for sunk costs, refunds and salvage.
Lifetime target: follow longer or add externally defensible remaining-value intervals. No point welfare claim from an optimistic terminal forecast. Publish sensitivity intervals even when uninformative. Preserve a distinction between logical partial-identification intervals and sampling confidence intervals.

3. Core treatments and timing
A=1 independent factual verification: reproduce asserted values against submitted source records and assumptions and report discrepancies; explicitly state whether denominator choice or metric relevance has also been checked. A=0 matched compilation of supplied evidence without independent checks. Same requested fields, packet length and allotted committee time. Measure actual staff time and delays rather than claiming burdens are exactly equal. Verification is a restricted factual service; its substantive mandate must be documented so a label does not conceal a broader intervention.
L=1 canonical metric governance: before the relevant information becomes available, lock an economically justified metric, population/denominator, baseline, horizon and source-version rules. Log all later exceptions. Enforceable data lineage or precommitted extraction is required. L=0 allow the normal documented choice among admissible measures with equal maximum packet size. A template that changes typography only is not the model's intervention.
Do not knowingly give committees false data, manufacture misleading statistics or ask real managers to falsify a project. Field arms compare permissible existing procedures with an enhanced check. The model's inflation option is a hypothesis, not an instruction to subjects. Report observed discrepancies separately from evidence of intentional deception.
Randomize equally across A0L0, A1L0, A0L1 and A1L1 within prespecified site/budget-band/calendar blocks, using an authorized independent assignment administrator. No assignment has been generated in this package.
Locked-register trial: assignment after opportunity census but before final case preparation measures policy effects conditional on the registered pool, including subsequent withdrawal. Prospective rollout: assign persistent clusters before opportunity generation and metric selection; use an independent opportunity census to capture entry and composition. These are distinct estimands; register lock after selection cannot test the full ex ante governance mechanism. If an acceptable comparison metric cannot be chosen independently of final quality, record that the model's governance technology is infeasible and do not claim to test it.

4. Confirmatory contrasts and analysis
Primary contrast: verification-by-metric-governance interaction on the 24-month net portfolio endpoint. It is a difference-in-differences between randomized arms, not a mediation estimate. Main verification and governance effects are secondary confirmatory contrasts.
Primary interaction tested two-sided at alpha .05 with a 95% interval. Secondary main-effect family uses Holm familywise .05; mechanism measures are exploratory with labeled effect intervals and Benjamini-Hochberg q-values. Do not replace the primary outcome with a significant process measure. Designate any regulatory or harm constraints before assignment.
Intention to treat includes noncompliance, undocumented metric exceptions and actual committee overrides. Cohort or persistent-cluster arm differences are the primary estimators. A saturated regression with block fixed effects and centered binary indicators estimates main effects and the interaction. Use design weights only if assignment or sampling probabilities require them. Budget-normalized outcomes use the predetermined denominator. Prespecify covariates for precision, never chosen from their outcome significance.
Randomization inference must preserve assignment blocks and persistent clusters. Use appropriate cluster uncertainty and disclose few-cluster limitations; project-level standard errors are invalid for budget-cluster assignment. Report all assigned clusters, all registered opportunities and all funding outcomes by arm. Future sharing of budget, reviewers or manager learning across clusters violates the simple no-interference argument and must trigger a design revision or a different estimand.

5. Power and economic thresholds
Equal-size independent homoskedastic clusters, 80% power, two-sided 5% normal approximation: main-effect MDE =5.6 sigma/sqrt(N), interaction MDE =11.2 sigma/sqrt(N), with N total independent clusters. These are planning formulas, not observed variances. At N=100,200,400,800 interaction MDEs are 1.120,.792,.560,.396 SD. A .2-SD interaction needs approximately 3136 independent clusters. If 1% of baseline budget is the minimum worthwhile net gain and residual SD is 5%, that is the .2-SD example. Stakeholders must declare an economically relevant threshold rather than accept it as calibrated.
Use authorized historical data and blinded variance estimates to refine prospective precision, accounting for unequal budgets, persistent managerial dependence and attrition. A small single-firm project stream likely cannot confirm the welfare interaction at useful precision. A pilot may establish data lineage, treatment separation and cost traceability, not a publication-level welfare effect. No-go for confirmatory welfare claims if expected uncertainty is wider than the declared decision threshold.

6. Mechanism and falsification
Record factual source discrepancies, metric revisions, denominator/population changes, omitted baselines, preparation effort and report provenance. Correct arithmetic with a selected measurement universe is different from factual inflation. Neither a discrepancy nor a score override alone proves political motive. Legitimate scope changes and private information are alternatives.
Mechanism prediction: under facts-only checking, detected falsification can fall while selection increases and allocation fails to improve. Joint governance may reduce both and improve net value. These are testable directional patterns, not unique proof of the model. A positive interaction could also reflect learning, process integration or reduced duplication. If governance compresses legitimate project heterogeneity or delays urgent decisions, count that harm.
Freeze predetermined sponsor-influence proxies from authorized pre-period rank, committee access and sponsorship-tie measures. Equal-weight standardized component index, with missing components handled uniformly and flags retained. Influence is access, not proven capture; it may correlate with valuable expertise. Do not use the index as an instrument for political power. No protected communication content is accessed without explicit authorization and a lawful data basis.
Independent baseline evidence ratings and shadow assessments may be noisy moderators only if measured before assignment without contaminating the committee. Funded-only sponsor/outcome regressions can be collider-biased. Do not control total effects for revised forecasts, entry, metric choice, preparation cost or funded scale when treatment can change them. Do not define evidence use by eventual success.

7. Selective outcomes and identifying assumptions
For randomized implemented policies, observe the full funded portfolio and policy costs in each arm. Under consistency, randomization, no cross-cluster interference and complete outcome measurement, arm contrasts identify implemented-policy average value. Hypothetical returns of every rejected project are unnecessary. Actual rejected implementation financed elsewhere changes the baseline and must be followed.
For historical off-policy project selection, rejected funded values are missing. Do not assume missing at random or set hypothetical values to zero. The manuscript's sharp bounds require a fixed register, fixed scale, additive project values, policy-invariant implementation and justified lower/upper values. Strong synergies or policy-induced value changes require joint portfolio bounds. A sign-ambiguous interval must remain sign-ambiguous.
Missing funded outcomes are a different problem: preserve IDs and missingness reasons, recover accounting losses where possible and apply prespecified bounds. Attrition is not cured by randomized assignment. Do not exclude failures.
Optional marginal funding lottery needs explicit separate authorization, genuine equipoise, appropriate governance and protected capacity. None is authorized or executed here. Known-probability randomized funding identifies value only within its supported marginal subset under consistency and relevant interference assumptions. Encouragement identifies a local complier effect only with relevance, exclusion and monotonicity, not all rejected values.

8. Separate sponsor-visibility protocol
If requested and feasible, randomize identified versus sponsor-blind initial review separately from the core two-factor study. Specify the exact attributes and prior-relevant history masked; a project may reveal its unit despite name removal. Measure reviewer identity guesses and leakage. Use the same contextual addendum and final review rights in both arms and analyze by assigned initial visibility. This identifies an initial visibility effect, not the causal effect of all political power or the removal of a free amount of soft information. The theory's visibility example reveals a genuine class-specific prior and requires case-by-case re-solution if conditional priors cross equilibrium regions.

9. Data access and feasibility
Private partner register, full evidence version history, extraction definitions, committee decisions, preparation/evaluation resource ledgers and implemented project cash flows are required. Employer affiliation does not authorize access. No data partner has been contacted or recruited. A signed agreement, legal basis, minimization, pseudonymized person map and retention controls are prerequisites.
Official SEC public portal https://data.sec.gov/ supplies aggregate disclosed financial data, not the required internal recipe and rejected-opportunity universe. Official Census MOPS https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/mops.html describes management-practice data; restricted-use access requires application via https://www.census.gov/topics/research/guidance/restricted-use-microdata.html . No access was obtained and those data are not asserted to contain the needed project fields. Public records do not resolve this design's selection problem.
Operational go/no-go targets: stable opportunity IDs; independent enforceable metric definition; clear separation of factual and relevance checks; >=95% funded cost/benefit traceability as a proposed operational threshold; acceptable leakage for any visibility study; enough independent clusters for declared precision. None of these are observed facts. A failed feasibility audit should produce a revised process-study estimand, not a fabricated welfare test.

R2 clarification, 17 October 2026. Proposed protocol, no implementation.
The full-range theoretical comparison conditions on an independent receiver-observed fallback value. No-evidence retains the prior-based optimal allocation and is not a zero-valued shutdown arm when the shock support crosses prior project value. A future no-evidence comparison must record the fallback opportunity and apply the common valuation rule to both funded and unfunded decisions. It avoids evidence-production and packet costs but may fund a project. Failed packets incur preparation, assessment and factual-check costs even when they lead to rejection or funding after detection. The uniform-cost boundary theorem rules out a net loss from costless auditing at any valuable unaudited open architecture under the stated technology. Thus higher selection costs are not alone evidence of lower welfare. Positive audit expenditure can create a net loss. The factorial interaction remains a prospective empirical contrast, not a globally signed prediction across all shock ranges. All process and resource accounts must distinguish gross information benefits, selection costs, review savings and audit expenditure. No additional study or contact is authorized by this clarification.

R3 clarification, 17 October 2026. This remains a prospective, unrun protocol.
The theoretical open-metric option now commits ex ante to independent random checking of each submitted positive packet with intensity tau. This adds no field authorization. A future implementation would need separately approved checking probabilities and a documented information-release partition. The theory pools checked-pass and unchecked-pass flags for the allocator before its funding decision, releasing only surviving positive, failed, or unchanged negative. Giving the allocator an audit-completion flag creates a different information structure and does not test the single-positive-posterior game. Cost billing/provenance records may be retained for later evaluation without selectively contaminating the allocation-stage information.
No extra arm, assignment, recruitment or real falsification was executed. Preparation is measured on every submitted packet, including failures; packet review costs attach to all submitted positives Q, and factual-check costs attach to completed checks, expected tau*Q. A checked accurate selected packet still consumes audit resources. A failed packet may nevertheless lead to funding if a measured fallback is worse. Private managerial control benefits are not investment welfare. A minimal deterrence probability is not assumed optimal; the theoretical global candidates include regime boundaries and all-submit stationary points. A remaining-inflation optimum does not establish that real-world deception is desirable. No broad priority, empirical prevalence or universal welfare claim is inferred from the synthetic cases.
